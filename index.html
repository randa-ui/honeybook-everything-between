Honeybook: Home assignment

A wedding photographer is hired for two moments: the first look and the last dance. The film opens there, close on the eyes, the ring, the kiss. That is the job.

Then it widens. Between those two moments sits the whole business: the inquiry that lands at two in the morning, the contract waiting for a signature, the invoice that has to be paid. In the film these appear as paper cards that orbit the photographer's moments and get handled one by one, without the photographer ever putting the camera down.

The visual language is borrowed from the photographer's own desk: polaroids, a camera, a bouquet, a cake topper, laid out on warm off-white paper, with a single accent colour marking every moment the product steps in.
